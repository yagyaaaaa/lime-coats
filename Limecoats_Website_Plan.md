## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — Why this redesign

The current limecoats.com is a 4-page brochure site (Home, About, Blog, Contact, Policy). It has good copy in places ("walls should do more than stand — they should inspire") but it loses to competitors on three things that decide whether a Mumbai architect, builder, or homeowner picks up the phone:

1. **No dedicated finish pages.** Every signature finish (Venetian, Stucco, Tadelakt-style, Travertine, Concrete, Metallic, Pitted, Drag, Pearl Silk, Colour Wash, Terrazzo, Flooring) deserves its own URL. Limocoat — the closest direct competitor and the brand most Mumbai architects already know — has a dedicated page for each finish (Concrete, Stucco, Venetian, Texture, Signature, Stencil, Natura, Smooth Concrete, Polymerised, Limowash, Limofloor). Right now if someone Googles "Venetian plaster Mumbai," Limocoat ranks. Limecoats doesn't even have the page.
2. **No project pages.** Limocoat has individual project URLs ("Stucco Lime Finish — Elegant Flat in Mumbai," "Zen Cafe Kala Ghoda — Concrete Lime Finish," "Ace Luxe 2019 — Signature Lime Finish"). Each one is a long-tail SEO magnet *and* a trust signal. You said you've done 10–20 projects but don't want to list them — we'll build a page structure that shows the work without naming clients (project type, location-by-area, finish used, photos, problem solved).
3. **No use-case pages and no audience-specific pages.** A homeowner in Bandra, an architect in Lower Parel, and a retail-chain procurement head in Andheri all need different things from the same site. Currently they all land on the same homepage with the same generic copy.

The new site fixes all three and adds the supporting pages needed to dominate Mumbai SEO for lime plaster — homeowner education content, architect/specifier resources, an FAQ, a Mumbai service-area page, and a clear visit-the-experience-centre page tied to your new Andheri location.

**Total pages:** **20** (final URL structure in Section 4).

### PAGE 3 — WHY LIME? (`/why-lime`)

**Purpose:** The deep education page. The page someone reads at 11pm before deciding to call you. Also the highest-value SEO page after the finish pages.

#### Section 3.2 — The 7 reasons (long form, each with sub-heading + 80–120 words)

**1. It absorbs CO₂ from the air.**
Lime plaster cures by re-absorbing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere — a process called carbonation. Over its lifetime, a lime-plastered wall pulls measurable amounts of CO₂ back out of the air around it. Acrylic paint does the opposite: it off-gasses VOCs into your home. Choosing lime is the rare luxury choice that's also the right environmental one.

**2. It breathes — so your walls don't trap damp.**
Mumbai's humidity is brutal. Cement and acrylic paint trap moisture inside the wall, which is why you see paint bubble, peel, and grow black mould patches by the third monsoon. Lime is vapour-permeable: water moves *through* it freely, in and out. The wall stays dry. The damp has nowhere to hide.

**3. It's naturally antibacterial.**
Lime is highly alkaline (pH ~12). Mould, bacteria, and dust mites cannot survive on it. This is not marketing. It is chemistry. For homes with children, asthma sufferers, or anyone who cares about indoor air quality, this matters.

**4. It contains zero VOCs.**
Volatile organic compounds — the chemicals that off-gas from new paint and give it that smell — are absent in real lime plaster. You can move into a freshly limed room the same day. No headaches. No "paint smell." No invisible chemical exposure for your family.

**5. It ages beautifully (instead of badly).**
Paint fades, peels, chips, and looks tired in 3–5 years. Lime develops a *patina* — a soft, luminous depth that the wall didn't have on day one. The walls of Indian havelis from 1850 still look better than apartment walls from 2020.

**6. It's the same material that built civilisation.**
Roman aqueducts. Indian havelis. Chettinad palaces. Renaissance frescoes. Moroccan riads. Lime is the oldest continuously-used building finish in human history, used for over 7,000 years. It works because it has had thousands of years to be perfected.

**7. It's fully mineral. Zero plastic.**
Modern paint is essentially plastic suspended in solvent. When you scrape it off a wall, you are scraping plastic. Lime plaster is calcium carbonate — the same stuff as limestone, marble, and seashells. When it eventually leaves a wall, it returns to the earth as the rock it came from.

#### Section 3.3 — India's Walls Are Changing (the cultural section)

#### PAGE 5 — Venetian Lime Plaster (`/finishes/venetian`)

**Primary keyword:** Venetian plaster Mumbai
**Secondary:** Venetian lime plaster India, polished plaster Mumbai, marmorino Mumbai, stucco veneziano

**Hero copy:**

- Sub: *The polished, marble-deep finish that built Renaissance Venice — handcrafted in our Andheri studio for Mumbai homes and brands.*

**What it is:** Venetian plaster (stucco veneziano) is a multi-layer lime finish, burnished to a polished, marble-like depth. Originated 1st-century Rome, perfected in 15th-century Venice for the city's lime-washed palaces. The signature is the way light moves across it — a soft luminosity that no flat paint can match.

**How we make it:** 3–4 layers of aged lime putty mixed with marble dust, applied with a steel trowel, each layer left to set before the next, then burnished with the trowel itself to compress the surface and bring up the shine. By hand. Days, not hours.

**Where it works best:** Living room feature walls, dining rooms, hotel lobbies, retail, master bedrooms, ceilings (yes — it's stunning on ceilings). Avoid in direct-shower wet zones (use Tadelakt for that).

**Colour & customisation:** Reference the Venetian palette tile from your catalog (page 6: muted lavender, soft grey, warm taupe). Custom matching available.

**Mumbai climate notes:** Venetian is fully breathable, so it handles humidity without bubbling. We seal exterior-facing Venetian walls with a lime-compatible mineral sealer to protect against salt air for SoBo and coastal projects.

**Project example:** *Penthouse, Lower Parel — full living room and dining ceiling in soft pearl-grey Venetian. Pairs with our Stucco finish on the bedrooms.*

**FAQs:**
- *How long does Venetian plaster last in Mumbai?* — 15–25 years with no refresh, develops patina.
- *Can it be cleaned?* — Yes, soft cloth + plain water; the burnished surface resists staining.
- *Is it more expensive than wallpaper / textured paint?* — Yes upfront. Cheaper over 25 years, because you don't redo it. (Don't quote rates — push to consultation.)
- *Can I do just one wall?* — Absolutely. A single Venetian feature wall is the most common Limecoats project.

## 8. WHAT YOU SHOULD NOT DO ON THIS WEBSITE (warnings)

- **Don't list client names** — you confirmed this. Stick to it. Use space-type + area framing.
- **Don't publish exact pricing** — quote on consultation. Pricing on a luxury craft service kills inbound (clients self-disqualify or use it to negotiate). Limocoat doesn't publish prices. Stucco Paint Art does — and that's because they're competing on price. You're not.
- **Don't use stock photos for the finish pages** — only your real work. Stock photography of "Venetian plaster" pulled from Italian sites is the single fastest way to lose architect trust.
- **Don't claim certifications you don't have** — you mentioned you don't have specific certifications yet. Don't fake them. Lead instead with the work, the artisans, and the material's intrinsic credentials (CO₂ absorbing, zero VOC, etc).
- **Don't compete with Asian Paints on volume or Limocoat on years-in-business** — you can't win those fights yet. Compete on craft, story, and authenticity. Those are fights you can win today.
